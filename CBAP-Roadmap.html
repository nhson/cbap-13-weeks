CBAP 3-Month Learning Roadmap (13 weeks × 10 h ≈ 130 h)

Context

A high-level CBAP study roadmap based on current (2026) exam facts: 3 months at 10 hours a week. Industry guidance is 120–160 study hours over 3–4 months, so 130 hours is on target, with little slack. It is the backbone of the 75-lesson course.

Exam facts (from the IIBA CBAP Handbook, May 2026)

  • 120 questions, 210 minutes. Multiple choice, scenario-based and case-study questions. You take it online with a remote proctor or at a PSI test center.
  • Based on BABOK® Guide v3. IIBA also lists the Business Analysis Standard as a study resource. There is no v4 exam yet.
  • Blueprint weighting:
#Knowledge Area%≈ Qs
1BA Planning & Monitoring (BAPM)14%17
2Elicitation & Collaboration (E&C)12%14
3Requirements Life Cycle Mgmt (RLCM)15%18
4Strategy Analysis (SA)15%18
5Requirements Analysis & Design Definition (RADD)30%36
6Solution Evaluation (SE)14%17
  • Eligibility: 7,500 BA work hours in the last 10 years, with at least 900 hours in 4 of the 6 KAs; 35 professional development (PD) hours in the last 4 years; 2 references.
  • Application and exam timing: the application is valid for 1 year, and you get up to 3 exam attempts in that year. Each exam purchase gives you 6 months to sit the exam.

Roadmap

Phase 0: Admin (week 1, runs alongside study)

  • Join IIBA. Membership gives you free access to BABOK v3 and the Standard on KnowledgeHub, and lowers the fees.
  • If you don’t have 35 PD hours yet, enroll in an IIBA-endorsed CBAP prep course. It covers the PD hours and fits into weeks 2–8.
  • Start the application and the BA Development Log now, because an audit can take time. Book the exam for about week 13 or 14 to lock in the deadline.

Phase 1: Foundation (weeks 1–2, 20 h)

  • BABOK Ch 1–2: the BA Core Concept Model (BACCM: Change, Need, Solution, Stakeholder, Value, Context), key terms, requirements classification and stakeholder roles.
  • Skim Ch 9 (Underlying Competencies) and the Ch 3–8 structure. Every task follows the same pattern: purpose → inputs → elements → guidelines/tools → techniques → stakeholders → outputs.
  • Take a diagnostic test (about 50 questions) to find your baseline and weak KAs.
  • Build an input/output flow map across all KAs. It’s the backbone of the “what comes next” questions.

Phase 2: Knowledge Area deep dive (weeks 3–9, 70 h)

Follow the BABOK lifecycle order. Study each KA’s related Ch 10 techniques in the same week.

WeekFocusKey outputs / techniques to master
3BAPM (14%)BA approach, stakeholder engagement, governance, information management, performance improvement. Techniques: stakeholder list/map/personas, RACI, estimation, lessons learned
4E&C (12%)Prepare, conduct and confirm elicitation; communicate BA information; stakeholder collaboration. Techniques: interviews, workshops, focus groups, observation, surveys, document analysis, prototyping
5RLCM (15%)Trace, maintain, prioritize and assess changes; approve requirements. Techniques: MoSCoW and other prioritization, traceability, backlog management, reviews, decision analysis
6SA (15%)Current state, future state, risks and change strategy. Techniques: SWOT, business model canvas, root cause/fishbone, benchmarking, gap analysis, business case
7–8RADD (30%, two weeks)Specify and model requirements, verify and validate, requirements architecture, design options, potential value and recommended solution. Techniques: data/process/use case/state/sequence modelling, user stories, acceptance criteria, non-functional requirements, glossary, data dictionary
9SE (14%)Measure performance, analyze measures, assess solution and enterprise limitations, recommend actions to increase value. Techniques: metrics/KPIs, balanced scorecard, vendor assessment

Every week in this phase ends with a 40-question KA quiz. Target ≥ 75% before moving on, and log every miss.

Phase 3: Techniques and perspectives consolidation (week 10, 10 h)

  • Build a 50-technique matrix: purpose, usage context and which KAs use each technique. Ch 10 accounts for about a third of question content.
  • BABOK Ch 11 perspectives: Agile, BI, IT, Business Architecture, BPM. They often appear in case-study context.
  • Re-read the Ch 9 Underlying Competencies.

Phase 4: Exam simulation and gap closing (weeks 11–13, 30 h)

  • Weeks 11–12: 2–3 full 120-question, 3.5-hour timed mocks under exam conditions. After each mock, spend about 3 h reviewing every wrong and every guessed answer against the BABOK text.
  • Focus on case studies: long scenarios where several answers look right and you pick the best one, the most “BABOK-correct” answer.
  • Week 13: one final mock of at least 80% as your go/no-go signal. Do light review only (input/output map, technique matrix, glossary) and rest before exam day.

Weekly rhythm (10 h)

  • Weekdays, 5 × 1 h: about 40 minutes of BABOK reading and notes, then about 20 minutes of flashcards or 10 practice questions.
  • Weekend, 2 × 2.5 h: techniques deep dive, the weekly quiz and error-log review.
  • Time split across the whole plan: about 55% reading and notes, 30% practice questions, 15% review and spaced repetition.

Recommended resources

  • Core: the BABOK® Guide v3 (read it at least once, fully) and the IIBA Business Analysis Standard.
  • Study guides (pick these two):
    • CBAP Certification Study Guide v3.0 by Richard & Elizabeth Larson (Watermark Learning). The most-reviewed CBAP guide, with plain-English explanations for each KA and task. Use it alongside BABOK in weeks 1–10.
    • CCBA® and CBAP® Certifications Study Guide by Esta Lessing (Packt, 2020), if you own it. Aligned to BABOK v3, with exam tips, scenarios and practice questions. Use it for KA-end review and weeks 11–13.
  • Question banks: use one quality simulator with case studies and at least 1,000 questions, such as Adaptive US or Watermark Learning/IIL, plus Udemy CBAP mock exams.
  • Community: your local IIBA chapter study group, which you can find through IIBA Chapter Events.

Milestones / checkpoints

  • End of week 2: diagnostic done, flow map drafted, application started.
  • End of week 9: every KA quiz at 75% or higher.
  • End of week 12: two full mocks at 75% or higher.
  • End of week 13: final mock at 80% or higher, then sit the exam.

Verification

  • Check progress with the weekly quiz scores and mock results. If a KA stays under 70%, move 2–3 hours from the next week to it.
  • If you’re below 70% on the full mocks by week 12, reschedule the exam. There is flexibility within the 6-month exam window.
